01

Aptamer-Guided Liposomal siRNA Delivery for Pancreatic Cancer Treatment

A targeted liposomal siRNA platform designed to recognize pancreatic cancer cells and suppress MTA2-mediated progression.

siRNA deliveryCancer treatmentAS1411 aptamerTargeted delivery
Laboratory

Nucleic Acid Biochemistry Laboratory

Supervisor

Dong-Eun Kim

Timeframe

Jun 2024 – Aug 2025

StatusPublished article ↗

Project Background

Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) is highly difficult to treat because its dense tumor microenvironment restricts drug penetration and reduces therapeutic efficacy. DNA aptamers are promising targeting ligands due to their ability to bind specific cell-surface proteins, while MTA2, a pro-metastatic regulator associated with PDAC progression, represents a potential therapeutic target for gene silencing.

Project Purpose

This project was designed to develop a targeted liposomal siRNA platform for PDAC therapy. AS1411 DNA aptamer was used to selectively recognize nucleolin-overexpressing PDAC cells, while MTA2 siRNA was encapsulated in liposomes to safely deliver gene-silencing cargo. Together, this strategy aimed to enhance PDAC-specific targeting and suppress MTA2-mediated cancer progression.

My Roles & Responsibilities

As the first author, I led the overall development and execution of this project.

  • Designed the research concept, established the hypothesis, and developed the experimental strategy.
  • Planned and conducted key experiments, including liposome preparation, cellular assays, molecular analysis, and functional validation.
  • Analyzed experimental data and designed graphical components for figures and scientific communication.

Workflow

  1. Selected AS1411 as a targeting aptamer based on its binding affinity to pancreatic cancer cells.
  2. Designed AS1411-conjugated liposomes encapsulating MTA2 siRNA for targeted PDAC therapy.
  3. Evaluated targeting efficiency using cellular uptake assays.
  4. Assessed targeted siRNA delivery and tumor-promoting gene-silencing effects.
  5. Evaluated therapeutic effects through cell-viability, apoptosis, and wound-healing assays.

Key Experiments

Detailed experimental data can be found in the published article linked above.

1. Aptamer screening

Selected an optimal DNA aptamer for PDAC cell targeting.

2. Liposome characterization

Evaluated particle size, morphology, and formulation stability.

3. Cellular uptake analysis

Confirmed selective uptake of AS1411-functionalized liposomes in PDAC cells.

4. Cytosolic siRNA release

Examined intracellular siRNA release and regulation of cancer-related signaling proteins.

5. Apoptosis assay

Evaluated anticancer activity by measuring apoptotic cell death.

Key Findings

  1. Aptamer-conjugated liposomes showed selective uptake in pancreatic cancer cells with negligible internalization in nucleolin-deficient cells.
  2. The aptamer-conjugated liposomal siRNA platform promoted efficient cytosolic release of siRNA.
  3. The platform achieved approximately 60% reduction of MTA2, robust restoration of PTEN expression, and approximately 50% suppression of AKT phosphorylation.
  4. These molecular changes resulted in approximately 50% lower cell viability, 35% higher apoptosis, and 75% lower cell migration.

02

Aerosolized PTEN mRNA Delivery Using Polymer-Liposome Complexes for Lung Cancer Treatment

Developing an aerosolizable polymer-liposome platform for localized mRNA delivery to lung cancer.

Aerosolized mRNA deliveryLung cancer therapyLocal pulmonary deliveryPolymer-liposome complex
Laboratory

Nucleic Acid Biochemistry Laboratory

Supervisors

Dong-Eun Kim, Keun-Sik Kim

Timeframe

Jul 2025 – Mar 2026

Status

Ongoing project

Project Background

Lung cancer remains one of the most lethal cancers worldwide. However, systemic delivery can cause off-target accumulation in organs such as the liver and spleen, limiting therapeutic efficacy and increasing toxicity. To address this limitation, we are developing an aerosolized polymer-liposome platform for localized mRNA delivery to lung cancer.

Project Purpose

This project aims to develop an aerosolizable pulmonary mRNA delivery platform for lung cancer treatment. By combining PTEN mRNA with polymer-liposome complexes, we are designing a local delivery system intended to enhance mRNA stability, cellular uptake, and tumor-suppressive signaling in lung cancer.

My Roles & Responsibilities

I led the development and execution of this project. My responsibilities included:

  • Designed the pulmonary mRNA delivery strategy and established the experimental hypothesis.
  • Planned and conducted key experiments, including complex preparation, cellular assays, molecular analysis, and functional validation.

Workflow

  1. Optimize the mRNA/polymer/liposome ratio to formulate stable complexes.
  2. Compare the physicochemical properties of optimized complexes with other liposomal conditions.
  3. Assess PTEN mRNA delivery efficiency and PTEN protein expression.
  4. Evaluate downstream therapeutic effects, including pAKT suppression and reduced cancer cell growth.

Key Experiments

Because this project is ongoing, only selected non-confidential data are shown.

1. Formulation ratio screening

Screened mRNA/polymer/liposome ratios to identify effective complexation conditions in lung cancer cells.

2. Formulation characterization

Compared the size and dispersity of candidate mRNA/polymer/liposome complex formulations.

3. Reporter mRNA expression

Measured intracellular reporter mRNA expression after treatment with candidate formulations.

4. Protein expression analysis

Analyzed PTEN upregulation and downstream pAKT suppression after PTEN mRNA delivery.

5. Apoptosis assay

Quantified apoptotic cell death to evaluate the functional effect of PTEN mRNA-loaded complexes.

Key Findings

  1. An optimal mRNA/polymer/liposome ratio was identified through flow-cytometric screening.
  2. The selected complexes retained favorable physicochemical properties after nebulization, based on internal comparative analyses.
  3. The optimized complexes enhanced mRNA delivery in lung cancer cells and increased PTEN protein expression by approximately 2-fold.
  4. PTEN mRNA-loaded complexes induced approximately 1.5-fold higher apoptotic cell death than liposome-mRNA formulations.

03

Functional Evaluation of Gold Nanoparticles as Drug Delivery Platforms for Pancreatic Cancer

Evaluating gold nanoparticle platforms for targeted drug delivery, anticancer response, and preliminary biocompatibility.

Cancer treatmentAS1411 aptamerTargeted deliveryGold nanoparticles
Laboratory

Nucleic Acid Biochemistry Laboratory

Supervisor

Dong-Eun Kim

Timeframe

Sep 2023 – Feb 2026

Status

Ongoing project

Project Background

Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma remains one of the most lethal malignancies, partly due to poor drug accumulation and stromal barriers that limit therapeutic efficacy. Although gemcitabine is widely used as a standard treatment, its clinical benefit is restricted by rapid metabolic inactivation and inefficient tumor delivery. This project focused on evaluating gold nanoparticle-based platforms as potential delivery systems to improve drug localization and therapeutic response.

Project Purpose

Using nanoparticles developed by a collaborator’s laboratory, I assessed their potential for selective cancer-cell targeting, anticancer drug delivery, and photothermal therapeutic enhancement in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma.

My Roles & Responsibilities

My role was to design and conduct biological experiments to evaluate the therapeutic potential of gold nanoparticle-based platforms.

  • Planned and conducted key in vitro and in vivo experiments, including cellular assays and animal toxicity evaluation.
  • Communicated with collaborators and presented experimental progress in regular project meetings.

Workflow

  1. Optimized aptamer conjugation conditions for selective cellular uptake.
  2. Quantified cancer-cell-targeted delivery using fluorescence-based assays.
  3. Evaluated the therapeutic effect of gemcitabine-loaded gold nanoparticles.
  4. Assessed systemic biocompatibility through in vivo toxicity analysis.

Key Experiments

Because this project is ongoing, only selected non-confidential data are shown.

1. Aptamer conjugation ratio & cellular uptake

Evaluated how aptamer conjugation levels affected cellular uptake and quantified uptake under the selected condition.

2. Cytotoxicity evaluation

Performed cell-viability assays to assess the therapeutic effect of gemcitabine-loaded gold nanoparticles.

3. Organ-level toxicity assessment

Examined potential organ-level toxicity following systemic administration using gross organ observation and H&E staining.

Key Findings

  1. AS1411-conjugated gold nanoparticles enhanced drug-associated fluorescence in nucleolin-positive cancer cells, supporting their potential for targeted delivery.
  2. Gemcitabine-loaded gold nanoparticles showed increased cytotoxic effects in pancreatic cancer cells compared with non-targeted or free nanoparticle groups.
  3. Systemic administration caused no detectable histological abnormalities in major organs under the tested conditions, supporting preliminary organ-level biocompatibility.
