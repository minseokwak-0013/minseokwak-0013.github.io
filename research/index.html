01

Aptamer-Guided Liposomal siRNA Delivery for Pancreatic Cancer Treatment

A targeted liposomal siRNA platform designed to recognize pancreatic cancer cells and suppress MTA2-mediated progression.

siRNA deliveryCancer treatmentAS1411 aptamerTargeted delivery
Laboratory

Nucleic Acid Biochemistry Laboratory

Supervisor

Dong-Eun Kim

Timeframe

Jun 2024 – Aug 2025

StatusPublished article ↗

Project Background

Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) is highly difficult to treat because its dense tumor microenvironment restricts drug penetration and reduces therapeutic efficacy. DNA aptamers are promising targeting ligands due to their ability to bind specific cell-surface proteins, while MTA2, a pro-metastatic regulator associated with PDAC progression, represents a potential therapeutic target for gene silencing.

Project Purpose

This project was designed to develop a targeted liposomal siRNA platform for PDAC therapy. AS1411 DNA aptamer was used to selectively recognize nucleolin-overexpressing PDAC cells, while MTA2 siRNA was encapsulated in liposomes to safely deliver gene-silencing cargo. Together, this strategy aimed to enhance PDAC-specific targeting and suppress MTA2-mediated cancer progression.

My Roles & Responsibilities

As the first author, I led the overall development and execution of this project.

  • Designed the research concept, established the hypothesis, and developed the experimental strategy.
  • Planned and conducted key experiments, including liposome preparation, cellular assays, molecular analysis, and functional validation.
  • Analyzed experimental data and designed graphical components for figures and scientific communication.

Workflow

  1. Compared cellular uptake of four candidate aptamers in pancreatic cancer cells and selected AS1411, which showed the highest uptake under the tested conditions.
  2. Designed AS1411-conjugated liposomes encapsulating MTA2 siRNA for targeted PDAC therapy.
  3. Evaluated targeting efficiency using cellular uptake assays.
  4. Assessed targeted siRNA delivery and tumor-promoting gene-silencing effects.
  5. Evaluated therapeutic effects through cell-viability, apoptosis, and wound-healing assays.

Key Experiments

Detailed experimental data can be found in the published article linked above.

1. Aptamer screening

Compared FAM-labeled AS1411, XQ-2D, AP1153, and M17 in MIA PaCa-2 cells by flow cytometry. AS1411 showed the highest cellular uptake among the four candidates.

2. Liposome characterization

Compared particle size and morphology across empty, siRNA-loaded, and AS1411-conjugated liposomes. AS1411-Lipm[siRNA] averaged 185.9 nm versus 136.4 nm for non-targeted Lipm[siRNA], an approximately 36% increase in mean diameter.

3. Cellular uptake analysis

Compared AS1411-Lipm with non-targeted Lipm and scrambled-aptamer SC-Lipm in MIA PaCa-2 and low-nucleolin L929 cells. AS1411 conjugation increased uptake in MIA PaCa-2 cells; antibody blocking supported nucleolin-mediated uptake.

4. Cytosolic siRNA release

Compared AS1411-Lipm[siRNA] with non-targeted Lipm[siRNA] at 1, 4, and 8 h. Lower siRNA–LysoTracker colocalization at 8 h supported enhanced endosomal escape. Relative to aptamer-free siRNA-loaded liposomes (Lipm[siRNA]), MTA2 protein expression was approximately 60% lower and pAKT was approximately 50% lower, while PTEN expression increased.

5. Apoptosis assay

In MIA PaCa-2 cells, the mean Annexin V-positive fraction was 32.34% with AS1411-Lipm[siRNA] versus 15.33% with non-targeted Lipm[siRNA]: approximately 111% higher, or 2.11 times the comparator value.

Key Findings

  1. AS1411-conjugated liposomes showed greater uptake in MIA PaCa-2 cells than non-targeted and scrambled-aptamer liposomes, without a comparable targeting advantage in low-nucleolin L929 controls.
  2. Compared with non-targeted Lipm[siRNA], AS1411-Lipm[siRNA] showed lower siRNA–LysoTracker colocalization at 8 h, consistent with enhanced endosomal escape.
  3. Compared with aptamer-free siRNA-loaded liposomes (Lipm[siRNA]), MTA2 protein expression was approximately 60% lower and pAKT was approximately 50% lower, while PTEN expression increased.
  4. Mean apoptotic-cell fractions were 32.34% versus 15.33% for AS1411-Lipm[siRNA] and non-targeted Lipm[siRNA], respectively—an approximately 111% relative increase.
  5. Cell viability was approximately 50% lower than the PBS-treated control after 24 h. Wound closure was approximately 75% lower than with plain liposomes (Lipm) at 60 h.

02

Aerosolized PTEN mRNA Delivery Using Polymer-Liposome Complexes for Lung Cancer Treatment

Designed and evaluated polymer-liposome formulations for PTEN mRNA delivery to lung cancer cells as part of a pulmonary delivery strategy.

Aerosolized mRNA deliveryLung cancer therapyLocal pulmonary deliveryPolymer-liposome complex
Laboratory

Nucleic Acid Biochemistry Laboratory

Supervisors

Dong-Eun Kim, Keun-Sik Kim

Timeframe

Jul 2025 – Mar 2026

Status

Ongoing project

Project Background

Lung cancer remains one of the most lethal cancers worldwide. However, systemic delivery can cause off-target accumulation in organs such as the liver and spleen, limiting therapeutic efficacy and increasing toxicity. To address this limitation, we are developing an aerosolized polymer-liposome platform for localized mRNA delivery to lung cancer.

Project Purpose

This project aims to develop an aerosolizable pulmonary mRNA delivery platform for lung cancer treatment. By combining PTEN mRNA with polymer-liposome complexes, we are designing a local delivery system intended to enhance mRNA stability, cellular uptake, and tumor-suppressive signaling in lung cancer.

My Roles & Responsibilities

I proposed and led the formulation design and cell-based evaluation for this pulmonary mRNA delivery project. My responsibilities included:

  • Designed the pulmonary mRNA delivery strategy and established the experimental hypothesis.
  • Screened 30 formulations and compared three assembly methods at a fixed composition.
  • Prepared complexes and performed physicochemical characterization, cellular delivery assays, protein expression analysis, and apoptosis assays before nebulization.

Further evaluation of post-nebulization performance, airway mucus transport, and in vivo delivery is ongoing in the laboratory.

Workflow

  1. Screened 30 formulations by varying the polymer-to-mRNA weight ratio and liposome-to-mRNA N/P ratio; selected a polymer-to-mRNA weight ratio of 0.5 and an N/P ratio of 2 for subsequent experiments.
  2. Compared three assembly methods at the same composition: simultaneous mixing; polymer–mRNA complexation followed by liposome addition; and combining polymer–mRNA complexes with liposomes by membrane extrusion.
  3. Compared particle size, polydispersity, and reporter protein expression across the assembly methods and reference formulations.
  4. Measured PTEN protein expression and pAKT, using Lipm-mRNA without polymer as the formulation comparator.
  5. Assessed apoptosis relative to Lipm-mRNA without polymer; the formulation and cell-based studies summarized here were performed before nebulization.

Key Experiments

Selected non-confidential results from my formulation and cell-based studies before nebulization are shown. Post-nebulization and in vivo evaluation is continuing in the laboratory.

1. Formulation ratio screening

Compared reporter protein expression across a 30-formulation screen. The strongest-response condition in the heatmap—polymer-to-mRNA weight ratio 0.5 and liposome-to-mRNA N/P ratio 2—was selected for subsequent experiments.

2. Formulation characterization

Compared particle size and polydispersity across Lipm-mRNA, polymer–mRNA complexes (PP), and LPP prepared by three assembly methods. LPP composition was held constant when comparing assembly methods.

3. Assembly method comparison

Compared simultaneous mixing, polymer–mRNA complexation followed by liposome addition, and membrane extrusion at a fixed composition. Particle size and EGFP protein expression varied across methods; Lipm-mRNA, PP, and Lipofectamine 3000 served as reference formulations.

4. Protein expression analysis

Before nebulization, LPP produced approximately twice the PTEN protein expression of Lipm-mRNA without polymer, an approximately 100% relative increase. Western blotting also compared pAKT with the same formulation control, with GAPDH as the loading control.

5. Apoptosis assay

The apoptotic-cell fraction was approximately 50% higher with LPP than with Lipm-mRNA without polymer—about 1.5 times the comparator value—as measured by Annexin V/PI flow cytometry in lung cancer cells before nebulization.

Key Findings

  1. A polymer-to-mRNA weight ratio of 0.5 and a liposome-to-mRNA N/P ratio of 2 were selected from the 30-formulation flow-cytometry screen.
  2. At a fixed composition, three assembly methods produced differences in particle size and protein expression.
  3. Before nebulization, the selected LPP formulation approximately doubled PTEN protein expression relative to Lipm-mRNA without polymer—an approximately 100% relative increase.
  4. The apoptotic-cell fraction was approximately 50% higher than with Lipm-mRNA without polymer, corresponding to about 1.5 times the comparator value.

03

Functional Evaluation of Gold Nanoparticles as Drug Delivery Platforms for Pancreatic Cancer

Evaluating gold nanoparticle platforms for targeted drug delivery, anticancer response, and preliminary biocompatibility.

Cancer treatmentAS1411 aptamerTargeted deliveryGold nanoparticles
Laboratory

Nucleic Acid Biochemistry Laboratory

Supervisor

Dong-Eun Kim

Timeframe

Sep 2023 – Feb 2026

Status

Ongoing project

Project Background

Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma remains one of the most lethal malignancies, partly due to poor drug accumulation and stromal barriers that limit therapeutic efficacy. Although gemcitabine is widely used as a standard treatment, its clinical benefit is restricted by rapid metabolic inactivation and inefficient tumor delivery. This project focused on evaluating gold nanoparticle-based platforms as potential delivery systems to improve drug localization and therapeutic response.

Project Purpose

Using nanoparticles developed by a collaborator’s laboratory, I assessed their potential for selective cancer-cell targeting, anticancer drug delivery, and photothermal therapeutic enhancement in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma.

My Roles & Responsibilities

My role was to design and conduct biological experiments to evaluate the therapeutic potential of gold nanoparticle-based platforms.

  • Planned and conducted key in vitro and in vivo experiments, including cellular assays and animal toxicity evaluation.
  • Communicated with collaborators and presented experimental progress in regular project meetings.

Workflow

  1. Optimized aptamer conjugation conditions for selective cellular uptake.
  2. Quantified cancer-cell-targeted delivery using fluorescence-based assays.
  3. Evaluated the therapeutic effect of gemcitabine-loaded gold nanoparticles.
  4. Assessed systemic biocompatibility through in vivo toxicity analysis.

Key Experiments

Because this project is ongoing, only selected non-confidential data are shown.

1. Aptamer conjugation ratio & cellular uptake

Compared aptamer conjugation conditions and Dox-associated fluorescence from AS1411-AuPS[Dox] versus scrambled-aptamer Sc-AuPS[Dox] in MIA PaCa-2, PANC-1, PC3, and L929 cells, with PBS and unloaded AuPS as reference groups.

2. Cytotoxicity evaluation

Compared gemcitabine-loaded AS1411-AuPS with gemcitabine-loaded scrambled-aptamer SC-AuPS and aptamer-free AuPS. Viability was normalized to untreated cells in each cell line (100%); unloaded AuPS was also evaluated.

3. Organ-level toxicity assessment

Compared major-organ appearance and H&E sections after saline, free gemcitabine, unloaded AuPS, or AuPS[Gem] administration. Saline served as the reference for the qualitative histological assessment.

Key Findings

  1. AS1411-AuPS[Dox] showed greater Dox-associated fluorescence than scrambled-aptamer Sc-AuPS[Dox] in MIA PaCa-2 and PANC-1 cells.
  2. Gemcitabine-loaded AS1411-AuPS reduced viability in MIA PaCa-2 and PANC-1 cells relative to gemcitabine-loaded aptamer-free and scrambled-aptamer formulations; viability was normalized to untreated cells (100%).
  3. No detectable histological abnormalities were observed in major organs relative to the saline reference under the tested conditions, supporting preliminary organ-level biocompatibility.
