Background & interests

Linking carrier design to in vivo fate and function

I earned my M.S. in Bioscience and Biotechnology from Konkuk University, where I developed lipid-based carriers for RNA delivery to tumors. I proposed and led two projects, an aptamer-functionalized liposomal carrier for siRNA delivery to pancreatic cancer and an aerosolizable polymer-liposome complex for mRNA delivery to lung cancer. Across both, I worked on formulation development and optimization, physicochemical characterization, and in vitro and in vivo evaluation.

Working on these projects moved my attention beyond the particle itself. A carrier meets a different biological environment depending on how it is administered, and it can also be altered by the processing it goes through before it reaches the body. I became interested in how those factors shape the in vivo fate of a carrier, and in how they can be reflected in the carrier from the beginning.

My goal is to develop lipid-based RNA carriers for cancer therapy that retain potency and selectivity in vivo, by treating route-specific biological barriers and processing conditions as design inputs from the earliest stage of formulation design. To do this, I want to separate observed in vivo behavior into biodistribution, cellular delivery, intracellular processing, and therapeutic function, and to build the relationships between them into mechanism-based design principles. In the longer term, I want to develop lipid-based RNA delivery platforms and advance them toward therapeutic translation.
