Background & interests

Linking carrier design to in vivo fate and function

I earned my M.S. in Bioscience and Biotechnology from Konkuk University, where I developed RNA delivery systems for cancer therapy. My research included an aptamer-functionalized liposomal carrier for pancreatic cancer–targeted siRNA delivery and a polymer–liposome complex for pulmonary mRNA delivery via nebulization. Through these projects, I gained experience in formulation development and optimization, physicochemical characterization, and in vitro and in vivo evaluation.

Building on this background, I am interested in the rational design of lipid-based carriers for mRNA and siRNA delivery, particularly for targeted and pulmonary applications. I want to tailor carrier properties to each RNA cargo, target tissue, disease setting, and route of administration while incorporating the biological context surrounding delivery into formulation design. Through this approach, I aim to develop more potent and reliable RNA delivery systems.

My long-term goal is to become an independent researcher who develops lipid-based RNA delivery platforms and advances them toward therapeutic translation. Through doctoral training, I hope to build the integrated expertise needed to connect formulation development with biological performance, ultimately contributing to more effective and clinically relevant RNA therapeutics for cancer.
